## What the law requires if TaalLab wants the U.S. section 512(c) hosting safe harbour

Under 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(2), a service provider seeking that safe harbour must designate an agent, publish the agent's name, address, phone number and email in a publicly accessible place on its service, and provide the designation to the U.S. Copyright Office. The Office maintains the public DMCA Designated Agent Directory.

The service provider must also keep the designation accurate and current. The Copyright Office's online designation expires after three years unless it is renewed by amendment or resubmission. Related legal entities need separate designations. Section 512(i) also requires a reasonably implemented repeat-infringer policy and accommodation of standard technical measures. The applicable notice-and-takedown and counter-notice requirements are in section 512(c)(3) and (g).

## Owner decision required

If Taal Lab wants to seek the U.S. section 512(c) safe harbour, the owner must decide the registering legal entity and agent, provide the agent's public contact details (including phone), approve the public disclosure, and complete the Copyright Office's online registration and payment. The designation must then be renewed at least every three years and updated promptly when details change.

If Taal Lab does not seek the U.S. safe harbour, it should still maintain a practical copyright complaint process and repeat-infringer policy, but must not describe its contact email as a registered DMCA agent or claim DMCA safe-harbour protection.

This is an operational research note, not a legal opinion. Obtain advice from U.S. copyright counsel before registration or making any safe-harbour claim.
