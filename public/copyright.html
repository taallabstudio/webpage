Copyright complaints

Last updated: 7 November 2026

Taal Lab respects copyright and expects users to upload, store and share only material they are entitled to use. This page explains how to contact us about content that may infringe copyright in files, transfers or OCEAN pages.

Important status note

We have not verified an active Taal Lab designation in the U.S. Copyright Office's DMCA Designated Agent Directory. Until the legal service-provider entity and any designation are verified, treat this page as a copyright contact route only; it does not claim that Taal Lab qualifies for the DMCA section 512 safe harbours or that the contact email is a registered DMCA agent.

1. Submit a copyright complaint

Email contact@taallab.in with the subject “Copyright complaint”. Include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature, or the signature of a person authorised to act for the copyright owner.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work you believe has been infringed.
  3. Identification of the specific material you believe is infringing and information that lets us locate it, such as the transfer link, page URL or relevant file name.
  4. Your name and contact details, including a working email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the disputed use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
  6. A statement that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorised to act for the owner of the exclusive right allegedly infringed.

We will review the information and may remove or disable access to material while a complaint is assessed. Please do not send passwords, payment-card details or unrelated personal information.

2. Request a review of removed material

If material you uploaded has been removed or disabled and you believe this happened because of a mistake or misidentification, email contact@taallab.in with the subject “Copyright review request”. Identify the material and its former location, explain why you believe access should be restored, and provide your contact details. We will review the request and may ask for supporting information. This is an internal review route, not a formal DMCA counter-notice procedure.

3. Repeat infringement

Accounts that repeatedly upload or share material in violation of copyright or other intellectual-property rights may be restricted or terminated, taking account of the circumstances and applicable law.
